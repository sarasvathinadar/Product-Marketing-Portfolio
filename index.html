Context & Challenge

SaaS discoverability in the age of AI search

Zoho Recruit and Zoho Forms operate in highly competitive SaaS categories. The challenge wasn't just traditional SEO — it was navigating a fundamentally shifting discovery landscape where AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) are increasingly the first point of contact between a buyer and a product.

The question: How do we ensure Zoho Recruit and Zoho Forms are visible, cited, and positioned correctly in both traditional and AI-driven search environments?

Product ICPs

Zoho Recruit: HR Managers, Talent Acquisition leads, and staffing agencies at SMB/mid-market companies. Zoho Forms: Operations, IT, and marketing teams needing no-code data collection tools.

My Role

Organic visibility strategy across search, AI, and app stores

I owned the multi-channel discoverability strategy for these two products — spanning traditional SEO, a newly-built AI/GEO framework, and full ASO for Android and iOS app properties.

Strategic Framework

Omni-channel Discoverability. As search behaviour fragments across Google, AI assistants, and app stores, product visibility requires a coordinated strategy across all three layers — not optimising each in isolation.

Strategy & Execution

AI-SEO & GEO: A new playbook for LLM visibility

I built Zoho's AI-SEO and Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) framework from scratch — a playbook for ensuring Zoho products surface in AI-generated answers and are correctly cited in LLM responses.

Key pillars: Brand citation strategy (ensuring Zoho appears in third-party authoritative sources LLMs train on), AI snippet optimisation, and LLM visibility tracking via Profound Analytics. This positioned Zoho ahead of the GEO curve — a channel most SaaS companies are only beginning to formalise in 2024–25.

ASO: Product discoverability on mobile storefronts

I led end-to-end ASO for Zoho Recruit and Zoho Forms on both Android and iOS. Execution: Metadata strategy (title, subtitle, description keyword optimisation), keyword targeting, and performance tracking via MobileAction and Play Console.

Traditional SEO & Reporting

Alongside AI-SEO, I maintained traditional on-page/off-page SEO for new and existing product pages — managing content calendars, tracking ranking trends via GSC and Ahrefs, and delivering weekly performance reports.

S2
Spec Project · Brand Storytelling · Ad Creative · Messaging Architecture
Beyond the Creator Brief: Storytelling, Brand Voice & Ad Direction at The Indus Valley
Deep-dive based on work at The Indus Valley
Narrative Framework
Ad Production
+
What this is

My core mandate at The Indus Valley was influencer and affiliate GTM (see Case Study 04). But the highest-leverage part of that work rarely shows up in a KPI dashboard — it was the storytelling and brand-voice discipline I brought to every deliverable that touched the customer. This spec deep-dive pulls that thread out on its own, because it's the part of the role that maps most directly to product marketing.

The Problem Underneath the Numbers

600+ creators, one brand voice

A creator network of this size is a messaging-consistency problem disguised as a scale problem. Every one of those 600+ creators has their own tone, audience, and content style — and every single piece of content they published was, functionally, a brand touchpoint. Without a clear narrative spine, that volume becomes noise rather than a coherent brand story.

My job wasn't just to approve or reject content. It was to give creators a story to tell, not just a product to mention — so that whether a video was a 15-second Reel or a 10-minute YouTube review, it reinforced the same underlying brand truth.

Building the narrative architecture

I developed a lightweight messaging framework that every brief was built around: problem → discovery → transformation → proof. Creators didn't just show a product; they told a before/after story rooted in a real, relatable frustration (patchy skin, unreliable haircare, decision fatigue in a crowded wellness aisle), which is what actually drives trust-based D2C purchase decisions.

Narrative Framework

Problem → Discovery → Transformation → Proof. Every creator brief and ad script was anchored to this arc, regardless of format or platform — giving 600+ independent voices one shared story.

Scriptwriting & Ad Production

From brief to finished ad

I wrote and adapted scripts for both organic creator content and paid ad creative — tailoring pacing, hooks, and CTAs to each platform's viewing behaviour. A YouTube review needed a slower, trust-building open; a Reels ad needed a hook within the first 2 seconds or it was scrolled past.

I worked directly with creators and the internal content team through the full production loop: brief → script draft → creator adaptation → shoot/edit review → compliance audit → publish — treating every ad the same way a PMM treats a launch asset: reviewed against a strategic brief, not just a creative one.

Testing narrative angles, not just creatives

Rather than only A/B testing thumbnails or CTAs, I tested which story angle converted best for different segments — e.g. "clinically-backed ingredients" messaging for skeptical first-time buyers vs. "everyday ritual" messaging for repeat-purchase, loyalty-driven audiences. Performance data from these tests then fed back into which narrative angle got scaled across the wider creator network.

What this demonstrates

This is positioning work at the execution layer — translating a brand's core value proposition into message variants for different buyer mindsets, then using data to decide which story wins. It's the same muscle a PMM uses to write messaging docs and sales narratives, just tested in public, in real time, across hundreds of creators.

Brand Governance as Storytelling Discipline

Protecting the story, not just the logo

Brand compliance is usually framed as a checklist — right logo, right claims, right disclaimers. I treated it as narrative protection. A technically "compliant" video that told the wrong story (e.g. leading with a discount instead of the transformation) still got sent back for revision, because it undercut the brand's positioning even without breaking a stated rule.

Cross-functional collaboration with the brand team

I worked closely with The Indus Valley's in-house social and brand team to keep creator-led storytelling aligned with the brand's own channel voice — contributing directly to content published on the brand's official Instagram, including collaborative reels that blended creator authenticity with on-brand visual identity.

What This Taught Me About Product Marketing
  • A messaging framework is only useful if it survives contact with 600 different voices telling it. Simplicity and repeatability matter more than cleverness.
  • Ad creative and organic creator content aren't different disciplines — they're the same story, tuned for different levels of trust and attention.
  • Brand governance at scale is a positioning function, not a legal one. The question isn't just "is this allowed," it's "does this reinforce or dilute the story."
  • Testing narrative angles (not just creative variants) is the highest-leverage optimisation lever in a creator-led GTM engine — it's messaging research disguised as performance marketing.
